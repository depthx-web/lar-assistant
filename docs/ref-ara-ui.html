2.3 Image acquisition

All participants were scanned on 3T systems using a T1-weighted magnetization-prepared sequence. Acquisition used a 1 mm isotropic voxel size, a repetition time of 2300 ms and an echo time of 2.98 ms at each of the four sites [4].

Scans were visually inspected for motion artefacts before processing. Cortical and subcortical volumes were estimated with an automated pipeline, and hippocampal volumes were normalised to total intracranial volume [7].

Follow-up scans were acquired at 12 and 24 months. Participants with fewer than two usable scans were excluded from the longitudinal analysis.

Hippocampal atrophy rates and cognitive decline in mild cognitive impairment

Target journal: Journal of Applied Neuroimaging|3,412 words
Status: Draft
Outline
Saved 14:32

Discussion

In this cohort, participants with mild cognitive impairment lost hippocampal volume faster than age-matched controls, in line with earlier longitudinal reports [3,7]. The difference was largest in the first twelve months of follow-up.

Annualised hippocampal atrophy of 3.1% therefore explains the faster decline observed in APOE ε4 carriers, and this effect is clearly the main driver of conversion to dementia.

Several limitations should be considered. Follow-up was restricted to 24 months, and scanner differences between sites may have added variance to the volumetric estimates.

Selected: Discussion, paragraph 2
OriginalAnnualised hippocampal atrophy of 3.1% therefore explains the faster decline observed in APOE ε4 carriers, and this effect is clearly the main driver of conversion to dementia.
SuggestionAnnualised hippocampal atrophy of 3.1% was associated with faster decline in APOE ε4 carriers. This association may contribute to conversion to dementia, although the present design cannot establish causation.
